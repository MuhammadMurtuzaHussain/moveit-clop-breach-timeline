The MOVEit Transfer / Cl0p mass-exploitation campaign

One unauthenticated SQL-injection zero-day in a single managed file-transfer product, exploited over a long weekend, ended up touching more than 2,700 organisations and roughly 96 million people — including employees in Ireland — without a single file being encrypted. This is the technical timeline, from the group's earlier dry runs to the final tally, with every claim linked to its source.

Why this one is worth studying

Reading the case study for CTI, not just for news

  • The pattern predates the incident. Accellion FTA (2020) → GoAnywhere MFT (Jan 2023) → MOVEit Transfer (May 2023) is the same actor running the same play against the same class of product: internet-facing, enterprise file-transfer software sitting at the edge of the network, trusted enough to hold sensitive data and exposed enough to be found by mass scanning.
  • No ransomware payload was needed. Cl0p shifted to exfiltration-only extortion: steal the data, threaten publication, skip the noisy and increasingly detectable step of encrypting everything. That changes what "containment" and "recovery" mean for defenders and for incident response plans built around ransomware playbooks.
  • Impact compounds through the supply chain. A single zero-day reached Aer Lingus through its payroll processor Zellis, and reached Ireland's HSE two hops further through EY's recruitment tooling — neither of those organisations ran MOVEit themselves.
  • Disclosure is not the end of the story. Two further SQL-injection flaws (CVE-2023-35036, CVE-2023-35708) surfaced in the weeks after the first patch, and the confirmed-victim count kept climbing for more than a year after exploitation stopped.
